You sign up for a "200 Meg" internet plan, but when you download a game on Steam, the speed only shows "25 MB/s." You might feel like you're being throttled, but in reality, you're just seeing the difference between two different units of measurement: **Megabits (Mbps)** and **Megabytes (MB/s)**.

### The Marketing vs. Reality Gap

Internet Service Providers (ISPs) almost universally advertise their speeds in **Megabits per second (Mbps)**. 
However, almost all software—including your web browser, Steam, and Windows—displays download speeds in **Megabytes per second (MB/s)**.

**The Math:** 
Because there are 8 bits in a byte, you must divide the ISP's number by 8 to get your "real world" download speed.
*   **40 Mbps** plan = **5 MB/s** download.
*   **100 Mbps** plan = **12.5 MB/s** download.
*   **1,000 Mbps (Gigabit)** plan = **125 MB/s** download.

### Bandwidth vs. Speed

It's helpful to think of your internet connection like a water pipe.
*   **Bandwidth (Mbps):** The **width** of the pipe. It determines how much data can fit through at once.
*   **Speed (Latency/Ping):** How **fast** the water is moving. It determines how quickly a single request gets to the server and back.

Having a "wide pipe" (high Mbps) is great for downloading large files or having five people stream Netflix at once. However, for online gaming, the "speed" (Ping) is more important than the bandwidth.

### What Speeds Do You Actually Need?

*   **5 Mbps:** Good for basic web browsing and one HD video stream.
*   **25 Mbps:** The FCC's current definition of "Broadband." Good for a small household with multiple devices.
*   **100+ Mbps:** Necessary for large households, 4K streaming, and frequent large file downloads (gaming).
*   **1,000 Mbps (Gigabit):** Overkill for most, but excellent for heavy users who want to download 100GB games in minutes.

### Why is my speed inconsistent?

If you have a 100 Mbps plan but your speed test only shows 50 Mbps, the culprit is often your **Wi-Fi**. Standard 2.4GHz Wi-Fi is prone to interference from walls, microwaves, and neighbors. For the most accurate speed, always test using an Ethernet cable plugged directly into the router.

### Conclusion

Next time you see an internet ad, remember the "Divide by 8" rule. By understanding the difference between Mbps and MB/s, you can accurately calculate how long that next big download will take and choose an internet plan that actually fits your needs.
