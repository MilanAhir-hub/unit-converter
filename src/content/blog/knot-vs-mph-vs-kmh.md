Speed is one of those measurements where the unit you use depends entirely on where you are. On a German Autobahn, it's **KM/H**. On a US highway, it's **MPH**. And on a sailboat in the middle of the Atlantic, it's **Knots**. Understanding the difference between these three is essential for travelers and navigation enthusiasts alike.

### 1. MPH (Miles Per Hour)
The standard unit for road travel in the US and UK.
*   **The Scale:** 60 MPH is the typical speed for a car on a highway.
*   **Usage:** Road signs, car speedometers (in the US/UK), and wind speeds in weather reports (in the US).

### 2. KM/H (Kilometers Per Hour)
The metric standard used by the vast majority of the world.
*   **The Scale:** 100 KM/H is roughly 62 MPH. 
*   **Usage:** Road travel in almost every country except the US and UK.

### 3. Knots (Nautical Miles Per Hour)
The standard for sea and air.
*   **The Scale:** 1 Knot = 1 Nautical Mile per hour.
*   **The History:** In the "Chip Log" method, sailors would throw a piece of wood into the water and count how many knots in a rope unspooled in 28 seconds. If 10 knots went out, they were going "10 knots."

### Key Conversions at a Glance

| **MPH** | **Knots** | 0.87 |

### Why Pilots and Sailors use Knots

As we discussed in our guide to the Nautical Mile, the "Knot" is tied to the Earth's latitude. If a pilot flies at 60 knots, they are crossing exactly one degree of latitude every hour. This makes the math of navigation significantly simpler than using miles or kilometers, which have no relationship to the lines on a map.

### Mental Shortcuts

*   **Knots to MPH:** Add 15%. (100 knots + 15 = 115 MPH).
*   **Knots to KM/H:** Multiply by 2, then subtract a little bit. (100 knots × 2 = 200... actual is 185 KM/H).
*   **MPH to KM/H:** The 1.6 rule. (60 MPH × 1.6 ≈ 96 KM/H).

### Conclusion

Whether you're watching a speedometer, a flight display, or a maritime chart, the "speed" you see is always a relationship between distance and time. By mastering the conversion between Knots, MPH, and KM/H, you'll always have a clear sense of how fast you're moving across the world—whether by land, sea, or air.
