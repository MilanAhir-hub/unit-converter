If you've ever tried to follow a European baking recipe in an American kitchen, you've hit the "Cup vs. Gram" wall. Most American recipes use **volume** (cups, tablespoons), while the rest of the world uses **weight** (grams, kilograms). This guide will help you bridge the gap and ensure your cakes always rise and your sauces are never too salty.

### 1. The Great Trap: Volume is not Weight

The most common mistake in cooking is assuming that "one cup" always weighs the same. 
*   **1 cup of Water** weighs 236 grams.
*   **1 cup of Flour** weighs about 125 grams.
*   **1 cup of Honey** weighs about 340 grams.

Because different ingredients have different **densities**, you cannot use a single conversion factor for everything. This is why professional bakers always use scales—it's the only way to be 100% consistent.

### 2. Common Dry Conversions (Approximate)

If you don't have a scale, use these common estimates for a standard US cup:

| Ingredient | 1 Cup in Grams | 1 Cup in Ounces |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **All-Purpose Flour** | 125g | 4.4 oz |
| **Granulated Sugar** | 200g | 7.1 oz |
| **Brown Sugar (Packed)**| 220g | 7.8 oz |
| **Butter** | 227g | 8 oz (2 sticks) |
| **Rice (Uncooked)** | 185g | 6.5 oz |

### 3. Liquid Measurements: The Easy Part

Unlike dry goods, liquids are much easier to convert because their density is usually close to water.
*   **1 Cup** ≈ 240 ml
*   **1 Tablespoon** = 15 ml
*   **1 Teaspoon** = 5 ml
*   **1 Quart** ≈ 1 Liter (actually 0.94L)

### 4. US vs. UK Cups

To make things even more confusing, a British (Imperial) cup is different from a US cup!
*   **US Cup:** 236.6 ml
*   **UK Cup:** 284.1 ml
*   **Metric Cup (Australia/Canada):** 250 ml

If your recipe comes from an old British cookbook, your "cup" is about 20% larger than a standard American one!

### 5. Oven Temperatures: Celsius to Fahrenheit

If your recipe calls for a "Gas Mark" or a Celsius temperature, use this quick guide:
*   **150°C** = 300°F (Slow)
*   **180°C** = 350°F (Moderate - most common baking temp)
*   **200°C** = 400°F (Hot)
*   **220°C** = 425°F (Very Hot)

### Conclusion

The secret to great cooking is precision. While "a pinch of this and a dash of that" works for soup, baking is a science. By understanding the relationship between volume and weight, and keeping a handy conversion chart in your kitchen, you can tackle any recipe from around the world with confidence. Happy cooking!
